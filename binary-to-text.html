How Binary Encoding Works

Computers store and process everything in binary—strings of 0s and 1s known as bits. Under the standard ASCII and UTF-8 encoding systems, each character is represented by 8 bits (one byte). For example, the capital letter 'A' is decimal 65, which corresponds to binary 01000001.
